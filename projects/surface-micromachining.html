Surface micromachining a suspended poly-Si bridge

I wrote a full process flow, 29 steps across four masks, for a 1 µm poly-Si bridge hanging 100 nm above an Al electrode coated in Si3N4. The hard part is not the deposition. It is choosing every etch so it removes one layer and leaves the others alone.

The decisions that make it work

  • PECVD nitride, not LPCVD. LPCVD nitride runs near 835°C, which would destroy the Al underneath. PECVD at 300°C keeps the metal intact.
  • Wet HF for the PSG pattern. A CF4/CHF3 plasma would chew through the 500 Å nitride at about 1900 Å/min. 10:1 HF etches Al at zero and nitride at about 3 Å/min, so a 13 s dip patterns the oxide and leaves the dielectric. The mask is oversized 100 nm per side to absorb the isotropic undercut.
  • HBr + Cl2 for the bridge. SF6/He would eat the PSG at 530 Å/min and expose the Al. HBr + Cl2 does not touch PSG at all, a perfect etch stop under the poly.
  • Poly-Si at 580°C. The bottom of the LPCVD window, chosen to protect the Al. At 60 Å/min, 1 µm takes about 2.8 hours.
  • 25:1 HF release, then supercritical CO2. 25:1 HF leaves Al and poly-Si untouched. Rinsing and air drying would let water's surface tension pull the bridge down across the 100 nm gap for good, so the drying step skips the liquid-gas interface entirely.
